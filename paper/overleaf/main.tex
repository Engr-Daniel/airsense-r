\begin{abstract}
Selecting an air-quality forecaster from clean validation accuracy does not establish whether that choice remains competitive when observations deteriorate or the monitoring site changes. We study this decision problem for one-hour-ahead PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting using hourly observations from 12 Beijing stations. Persistence, XGBoost, a multilayer perceptron (MLP), and a compact gated recurrent unit (GRU) network are compared under a frozen chronological protocol with training-only tuning, a predefined 1~$\mu$g/m$^3$ practical near-tie rule, and paired temporal-block uncertainty. Primary synthetic interventions cover non-target feature dropout, channel loss, measurement noise, and contiguous outages; historical PM$_{2.5}$ is not synthetically corrupted in this primary experiment. Site shift is evaluated through 12 leave-one-station-out folds. Although XGBoost has the lowest clean-test mean absolute error (MAE) of 9.711~$\mu$g/m$^3$, the validation rule selects persistence, whose clean-test selection regret is 0.569~$\mu$g/m$^3$ (95\% confidence interval (CI): 0.340--0.763). Persistence remains within the practical threshold in all 15 full-period stress conditions under each imputation policy. Mean station-specific regret under site holdout is 0.550~$\mu$g/m$^3$ (95\% CI: 0.400--0.739), with 11 of 12 station point estimates within the practical threshold. A separately declared post-results extension probes the protected target-history assumption. Under a 12-hour PM$_{2.5}$ outage, affected-window regret rises to 2.282~$\mu$g/m$^3$ (95\% CI: 1.696--2.910), despite full-period regret of 0.651. These findings support a bounded conclusion: validation-based selection can remain practically competitive under many deployment stresses, but reliability depends on target-history availability and on whether evaluation is global or focused on affected windows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Ambient PM$_{2.5}$ remains a major environmental and public-health concern, with a substantial global disease burden attributable to long-term exposure~\cite{sang2022pm25burden}. Reliable short-horizon forecasting is therefore relevant to environmental monitoring and operational decision support. Yet a forecasting comparison ultimately produces a deployment decision, not merely a leaderboard.

That distinction matters because model rankings can change under missing data, measurement degradation, or distribution shift~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide,liu2024distshift,han2024selection}. A rank reversal alone, however, does not show that the original model choice has become materially poor. The deployment-relevant question is instead: \emph{how costly is it to retain the model selected under clean validation when operating conditions change?}

\AirSense{} studies that question through \emph{selection regret}, a decision-oriented quantity also used in recent robust model-selection work~\cite{semper2026dss}: the excess forecasting error of the validation-selected family relative to the lowest-error candidate under the same evaluation condition. This paper does not introduce a new forecasting architecture. Its contribution is a reproducible empirical evaluation of a frozen accuracy-and-simplicity selection rule across clean testing, controlled input degradation, and unseen-station evaluation.

The study makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item evaluation of a frozen validation-based selection rule across complementary deployment stresses;
\item interpretation of rank changes through selection regret, practical near-ties, and paired uncertainty;
\item a bounded post-results checkpoint that directly tests the protected PM$_{2.5}$ target-history assumption; and
\item an auditable evidence chain linking protocol, predictions, model lineage, uncertainty, and manuscript claims.
\end{enumerate}

The claims are deliberately bounded. We do not claim universal persistence superiority, a validated model-switching policy, robustness to physical sensor failure, or inter-city transfer.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}

\subsection{Forecasting With Incomplete Air-Quality Observations}
Missing observations and operational gaps are established problems in air-quality sensing. Chowdhury \emph{et al.} show that imputation strategy can materially affect PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting under incomplete sensor data~\cite{chowdhury2026imputation}. Alvarado-Alcon \emph{et al.} study sparse sensor networks, cross-location generalization, and missing streams, including the greater difficulty of structured outages compared with randomly distributed missingness~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide}. Yagnik \emph{et al.} likewise emphasize leakage-safe, mask-aware short-horizon PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting and benchmark persistence, tree, recurrent, and graph-based models on held-out periods~\cite{yagnik2026leakage}.

These studies establish that missing-data handling and cross-location forecasting are already active research areas; neither is claimed as novel here.

\subsection{Model Assessment Under Distribution Shift}
Model assessment itself becomes difficult when deployment conditions differ from development conditions. Liu \emph{et al.} show that PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting architectures can respond differently to temporal distribution shift~\cite{liu2024distshift}. Han \emph{et al.} formalize model assessment and selection under temporal shift and explicitly treat model comparison as a problem in a changing environment~\cite{han2024selection}.

The closest methodological overlap is Semper \emph{et al.}~\cite{semper2026dss}, who reevaluate fixed prediction bundles under perturbed evaluation references and quantify post-training selection regret. \AirSense{} instead perturbs the chronological \emph{input observation stream}, regenerates forecasts, and separately excludes monitoring stations from model development.

\subsection{Research Gap and Positioning}
The gap addressed here is narrower than robust air-quality forecasting. We ask whether a family selected under clean validation remains practically defensible after the observation stream degrades or the deployment site changes. The contribution is therefore an empirical decision-reliability study that combines prespecified validation selection, controlled input interventions, station exclusion, selection regret, and dependence-aware uncertainty. We introduce no new forecasting architecture and make no claim to the first use of selection regret.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Dataset and Forecasting Task}
We use the UCI Beijing Multi-Site Air Quality dataset~\cite{uci_beijing}. The repository audit records 420,768 hourly rows across 12 monitoring stations, with 35,064 rows per station. The timestamp grid is complete, but measurements are not: accepted audit evidence records 8,739 missing PM$_{2.5}$ values (2.08\%) and 20,701 missing CO values (4.92\%).

The target is PM$_{2.5}$ concentration one hour ahead using the preceding 24 chronological hours. Numeric inputs are PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, SO$_2$, NO$_2$, CO, O$_3$, temperature, pressure, dew point, rainfall, and wind speed; wind direction is categorical. Deterministic cyclical features encode hour-of-day and day-of-year. Station identity is used for grouping and exclusion, not as a predictor.

The chronological target splits are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item training: 2 March 2013--28 February 2015;
\item validation: 1 March 2015--29 February 2016; and
\item final test: 1 March 2016--28 February 2017.
\end{itemize}
Missing targets are not imputed or scored, and synthetic interventions never alter the evaluation labels. The clean final-test comparison contains 102,753 distinct station--target observations shared by every family and training seed. Missing inputs do not remove otherwise eligible targets. A target may use preceding observations from the previous split because they occur strictly before its forecast time; learned preprocessing remains fitted only on training data.

\subsection{Leakage-Controlled Temporal Evaluation}
Forecast evaluation follows chronological rather than randomly shuffled partitions. Out-of-sample design is central to time-series studies because temporal dependence and non-stationarity complicate the interpretation of conventional random splits~\cite{tashman2000oos,bergmeir2018cv}. Hyperparameters are tuned only inside the training period using three expanding-window folds. Clean validation data then determine the model family, while the final test period was reserved from feature design, hyperparameter tuning, model-family selection, primary corruption-severity selection, and uncertainty-setting choices. This chronology describes the primary protocol; the target-history and tolerance analyses were declared after primary results and are labeled post-results.

For a target at time $t+1$, the forecasting pipeline uses only observations available through time $t$. Missing target PM$_{2.5}$ values are never imputed or scored. The 24-hour lookback is a prespecified modeling choice intended to retain one daily cycle while keeping the experiment bounded; it is not claimed to be globally optimal.
\subsection{Causal Preprocessing}
Each raw sensor channel receives a missingness indicator before imputation. Within each station, causal forward fill is limited to six consecutive hours. Remaining numeric gaps use medians estimated on fitting data only. Wind direction uses training-derived categories with explicit missing and unseen representations. MLP and GRU numeric inputs are standardized from training statistics after imputation; XGBoost receives unscaled numeric inputs.

A sensitivity policy removes forward fill while retaining training-only fallback and missingness indicators. The purpose is to test whether conclusions are primarily an artifact of causal carry-forward~\cite{chowdhury2026imputation}.

\subsection{Candidate Model Architectures}
The candidate set intentionally spans four levels of modeling complexity. Persistence predicts the next PM$_{2.5}$ value from the latest causally preprocessed PM$_{2.5}$ observation. XGBoost uses the flattened 24-hour feature history plus four calendar features~\cite{chen2016xgboost}. The MLP uses the same flattened history and calendar features with two ReLU dense layers. The GRU preserves the 24-hour sequence, encodes it with a recurrent unit, concatenates the four calendar features at the output head, and produces a linear scalar forecast~\cite{cho2014gru}.

Learned models use three fixed training seeds (42, 31415, and 27182). Persistence is deterministic. The bounded search is part of the experimental scope and is not presented as evidence that the chosen hyperparameters are globally optimal.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Tuning and Validation Selection}
The three internal folds fit through 28 February, 30 June, and 31 October 2014, respectively, and validate on March--June 2014, July--October 2014, and November 2014--February 2015. Fit cutoffs end at 23:00; validation starts at 00:00 on the next day. Training-target eligibility begins at the fixed training boundary; preceding observations supply the initial lookback. Candidate configurations minimize MAE averaged over training seeds, stations and folds with equal weights; exact ties use the declared candidate order. Each chosen configuration is refitted on the full training period before clean validation. Neural models use mean-squared-error loss, batch size 512, and a fixed 20-epoch budget without early stopping. Validation observations are not added to the final fitting period.

Candidate families are evaluated on the same clean validation rows. The lowest aggregate validation MAE is identified. If another family lies within 1~$\mu$g/m$^3$ of the best, the difference is treated as a practical near-tie and the simpler family is preferred according to the fixed order
\[
\text{Persistence} \succ \text{XGBoost} \succ \text{MLP} \succ \text{GRU}.
\]
The selected family is frozen before final-test and stress outcomes are interpreted. The near-tie set includes the minimum-MAE family; when it has only one member, that family is selected. The ordering is a declared simplicity preference, not an experimentally measured ranking of inference cost.

For evaluation condition $s$, selection regret is
\begin{equation}
R_s = \mathrm{MAE}_s(m_{\mathrm{sel}})-\min_m\mathrm{MAE}_s(m),
\end{equation}
where $m_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is the validation-selected family. Regret measures model-choice cost relative to the available alternatives; it does not establish absolute operational adequacy.

\subsection{Sensor-Degradation Protocol}
After the clean-validation decision is frozen, no candidate is retrained for stress evaluation. Corruption is applied to the chronological observation stream before overlapping windows are constructed, so a physical observation is corrupted consistently wherever it appears.

The primary intervention families are 10/30/50\% random non-target feature dropout; complete loss of NO$_2$, CO, or temperature; 5/10/20\% normalized Gaussian measurement noise; and 6-hour or 12-hour contiguous outages of NO$_2$, CO, or temperature. Historical PM$_{2.5}$ is intentionally protected in this primary experiment. Structured outages are summarized both over the full test period and over forecast windows whose histories intersect the outage because full-period averages can dilute short operational failures~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide}.

The five corruption seeds are 101, 202, 303, 404, and 505. Random dropout is independent Bernoulli masking of station--timestamp--channel entries across the non-target channels, including wind direction. Noise excludes categorical wind direction and has channel-specific standard deviation equal to severity times the training interquartile range divided by 1.349; a zero interquartile range uses the training standard deviation. Naturally nonnegative pollutant and weather channels are clipped at zero after injection (PM$_{10}$, SO$_2$, NO$_2$, CO, O$_3$, rainfall and wind speed). Each outage condition samples one seeded start per station per test calendar month, with its full duration inside that month. All model families and training seeds share the same realization and eligible targets.

\subsection{Unseen-Station Evaluation}
Cross-site generalization is evaluated with 12 leave-one-station-out (LOSO) folds. In each fold, the held-out station is excluded from preprocessing-statistic estimation, hyperparameter tuning, model fitting, and clean-validation selection. The selected family is then evaluated only on final-test targets from the excluded station. Every fold retains the same chronological training, validation and test boundaries; future observations from the other 11 stations are never admitted into fitting. Fold-specific configuration selection and fitting are repeated, so differences from the pooled benchmark are not a causal estimate of site shift alone.

Recent local observations from the held-out station remain available at inference, so the experiment measures within-Beijing unseen-station forecasting rather than prediction at a completely uninstrumented location. This distinction is important when comparing cross-location studies because available local information can materially change the task definition~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide}.

\subsection{Post-Results Sensitivity Analyses}
After the primary confirmatory evidence had been inspected and preserved, a bounded extension was declared to probe the protected target-history assumption. The accepted models and preprocessing remain fixed. Exactly five conditions are tested: PM$_{2.5}$ dropout at 10, 30, and 50\%, and PM$_{2.5}$ contiguous outages of 6 and 12 hours. All candidates receive the same corrupted chronological stream before window construction; evaluation labels remain untouched. The extension uses primary imputation only and the same five corruption seeds and outage schedule. Persistence uses the resulting imputed PM$_{2.5}$ history, without access to an uncorrupted copy. The extension is explicitly post-results and is reported separately from the original confirmatory evidence.

A separate post-results tolerance analysis considers 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0~$\mu$g/m$^3$. It separates reinterpretation of the original selection's regret from counterfactual choices made using saved validation scores alone. The original 1.0 rule remains primary; no threshold is selected from test performance. Alternative-policy outcomes are descriptive point estimates, without newly computed intervals.

\subsection{Metrics, Statistical Uncertainty, and Reproducibility}
MAE is the primary metric; RMSE and $R^2$ are secondary. Metrics are calculated within each station and training seed, averaged across training seeds, then across corruption realizations when applicable, and finally across stations with equal weight. These are averages of model-specific metrics, not scores of an ensemble prediction. Thus the reported aggregate RMSE is not obtained by pooling all squared errors. Relative performance degradation is $100(M_s-M_{\mathrm{clean}})/M_{\mathrm{clean}}$; the clean comparator uses the same target mask as the stressed scope. Kendall's tau-b summarizes numerical rank agreement, not practical equivalence.

For pooled conditions, selection regret subtracts the lowest aggregate candidate MAE. For LOSO, regret is calculated separately within each held-out station and then averaged equally across the 12 stations; it is not a difference against one globally best family.

Paired uncertainty uses a calendar-aligned, 24-hour moving-block bootstrap with 2,000 replicates, seed 424242, and percentile 95\% intervals~\cite{kuensch1989bootstrap}. Block starts are sampled with replacement from valid calendar-hour starts; concatenated blocks span the test duration, truncating the final block if necessary. Identical sampled hours are used across candidates, stations and corruption realizations, retaining observed-label and affected-window masks. Each replicate recomputes station metrics and the retrospective minimum; the validation-selected family remains fixed. LOSO replicates average station-specific regrets. Sparse affected-window replicates with an empty station/realization yield an unavailable interval rather than an unreported redraw.

Intervals are conditional on the observed period, stations, fitted training seeds and corruption-seed set; neither fitting nor corruption generation is repeated inside the bootstrap. They are pointwise, not simultaneous across conditions, and the fixed block length does not guarantee coverage of longer dependence scales. Near-tie membership is a practical interpretation, not a statistical equivalence test.



\section{Results}
\subsection{Clean Benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:clean} reports final-test performance. XGBoost achieves the lowest MAE at 9.7109~$\mu$g/m$^3$, followed by persistence at 10.2796, MLP at 10.8102, and GRU at 15.9574. The validation rule had already selected persistence. The resulting clean-test regret is 0.5688~$\mu$g/m$^3$ with a 95\% paired CI of 0.3400--0.7632.

The paired interval excludes zero, so XGBoost's numerical advantage over persistence is resolved under the study's bootstrap. However, the entire interval remains below the prespecified 1~$\mu$g/m$^3$ practical tolerance. The clean benchmark therefore illustrates the central distinction between exact rank and the cost of retaining the original selection.

\subsection{Non-Target Sensor Degradation}
Across all 15 full-period degradation conditions, persistence remains within the practical regret threshold under both the primary and no-forward-fill sensitivity policies. The maximum full-period regret is approximately 0.569~$\mu$g/m$^3$ under the primary policy and 0.552~$\mu$g/m$^3$ under the sensitivity policy. Numerical rankings can change without making the original selection materially poor.

This conclusion is deliberately conditional: historical PM$_{2.5}$ is protected in the primary stress experiment, so the result does not demonstrate robustness to failure of the target-history channel.

\subsection{Unseen-Station Evaluation}
Across 12 LOSO folds, persistence is selected in every fold by the frozen rule. Mean station-specific regret is 0.5504~$\mu$g/m$^3$ (95\% CI: 0.4004--0.7388), and 11 of 12 station point estimates lie within the practical threshold. Shunyi has the largest point regret at 1.0504~$\mu$g/m$^3$ with a 95\% CI of 0.7417--1.3109. The evidence therefore supports generally small within-Beijing selection cost, while also showing that cross-site reliability is not uniform.

\subsection{Target-History Checkpoint}
The post-results extension sharpens the deployment interpretation. Under PM$_{2.5}$ dropout at 10, 30, and 50\%, persistence is the lowest-error candidate among the four and therefore has zero selection regret. Yet low regret does not imply strong absolute accuracy: persistence MAE reaches 15.671~$\mu$g/m$^3$ at 50\% dropout.

Contiguous outages reveal a different boundary. Under a 6-hour PM$_{2.5}$ outage, full-period persistence regret is 0.557~$\mu$g/m$^3$ and affected-window regret is 0.198. Under a 12-hour outage, full-period regret is 0.651, but affected-window regret rises to 2.282~$\mu$g/m$^3$ with a 95\% CI of 1.696--2.910, entirely above the primary practical threshold. The key result is therefore not simply that target-history degradation matters, but that full-period averaging can hide a localized selection failure in forecasts whose 24-hour input histories intersect the outage. This scope can extend beyond the outage itself, after observations resume.

\subsection{Tolerance Sensitivity}
Interpretation is sensitive to the practical tolerance. At 0.5~$\mu$g/m$^3$, the clean point regret exceeds the tolerance, only 8 of 15 full-period sensor conditions lie within it under each policy, and 5 of 12 LOSO point regrets lie within it. At the primary 1.0 threshold, these counts are 15/15 and 11/12, respectively. In contrast, counterfactual clean selection changes little: persistence remains the pooled clean choice across tolerances of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0~$\mu$g/m$^3$ because its validation gap to XGBoost is approximately 0.3688. At 0.5, the Nongzhanguan LOSO fold instead selects XGBoost from saved validation scores; mean station-specific regret under that counterfactual policy is 0.4978 rather than 0.5504. Original-policy confidence intervals are not reused for this changed selection. The practical judgment is therefore more threshold-sensitive than the pooled selection itself.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Rank Stability Versus Decision Stability}
The central result is not that persistence is universally best. XGBoost is the numerical clean-test winner, and the exact winner changes across some conditions. The stronger interpretation is that a validation-selected simple model can remain practically competitive even when its rank changes, provided the excess error relative to the available alternatives remains small.

This interpretation is consistent with the broader argument that model assessment under changing distributions should be treated explicitly rather than inferred from a single static validation ranking~\cite{han2024selection,semper2026dss}. \AirSense{} contributes an input-perturbation and station-exclusion perspective to that decision problem.

\subsection{Why the Target-History Checkpoint Matters}
The primary sensor-degradation result could be misread as evidence that persistence is generally robust to sensor failure. That would be too broad because persistence depends directly on recent PM$_{2.5}$, which was protected in the primary intervention set. The post-results checkpoint clarifies the boundary: random PM$_{2.5}$ dropout does not create regret relative to the tested alternatives, but a 12-hour contiguous outage produces a materially larger affected-window penalty. This pattern also reinforces the distinction between random missingness and structured outages emphasized in recent air-quality forecasting work~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide}.

\subsection{Deployment-Oriented Interpretation}
The results do not provide a universal instruction to deploy persistence. Instead, they support a decision framework: determine whether the selected family remains close to the best available alternative under plausible observation failures and at new sites, and evaluate the periods directly exposed to the failure rather than relying only on a global average.

Low selection regret also does not establish acceptable absolute forecasting accuracy. A model can have low regret because all candidates perform poorly. Conversely, high regret identifies a costly selection under a condition but does not validate an adaptive switching or fallback policy.

\subsection{Relationship to Prior Work}
Compared with studies that primarily optimize architecture or imputation strategy~\cite{chowdhury2026imputation,yagnik2026leakage}, \AirSense{} treats the predeployment family choice as the object of evaluation. Compared with Semper \emph{et al.}~\cite{semper2026dss}, perturbations occur in the model inputs and produce new forecasts rather than only changing evaluation references. Compared with cross-location forecasting work~\cite{alvaradoalcon2026citywide}, the LOSO task here is intentionally narrower because recent local history from the held-out station remains available at inference.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
The evidence comes from one historical Beijing dataset, one forecast horizon, and four candidate families. A linear or ridge model with lag features is absent, and the two-configuration search per learned family does not establish architecture-level optimality. The fixed simplicity order is not a measured cost--accuracy frontier. The synthetic interventions are controlled stress tests rather than validated physical fault models. The within-city LOSO design does not establish inter-city or cross-climate transfer. The 1~$\mu$g/m$^3$ tolerance is a prespecified modeling decision threshold rather than a health or regulatory standard, and the sensitivity analysis shows that conclusions become less favorable under a stricter 0.5~$\mu$g/m$^3$ interpretation.

The target-history checkpoint is explicitly post-results and therefore should not be assigned the same confirmatory status as the original protocol. Uncertainty intervals are also conditional on this dataset, these fitted seeds, and the chosen 24-hour block bootstrap. Finally, selection regret is relative to the tested alternatives: low regret can coexist with poor absolute accuracy, while high regret does not itself validate a switching policy.

Future work should test the framework on additional cities and climates, longer forecasting horizons, broader candidate sets, and real low-cost-sensor fault logs. A separate prospective study could evaluate adaptive fallback or model-switching policies, but such policies should be tested directly rather than inferred from retrospective regret.

\section{Reproducibility and Availability}
A preprocessing serialization defect was identified after initial clean-test inspection: sorted statistic mappings changed scaled feature order on reload. The corrected evaluation reused the original trained models, hyperparameters and validation selection, with development-prediction replay before corrected test scoring. An additional CSV round-trip correction preserved decimal target values. Original invalidated outputs and the correction chronology are retained; only independently accepted corrected results are reported. No retuning, refitting or relaxation of replay tolerances was used. These safeguards do not erase the post-results timing of the corrections.

The repository preserves the frozen protocol, declared search spaces, preprocessing logic, prediction artifacts, uncertainty settings, executed notebooks, and independent verification receipts. Primary evidence and post-results sensitivity analyses are stored separately so later analyses do not overwrite the original experiment. Code and compact evidence are available at \url{https://github.com/Engr-Daniel/airsense-r}. The dataset is available from UCI~\cite{uci_beijing}. Large native models and prediction arrays currently reside in project Drive storage with public checksum references; public archival access to those large files remains a submission task.

\section{Conclusion}
\AirSense{} evaluates the reliability of a model-selection decision rather than only the stability of a leaderboard. Within the declared candidate set and study boundary, the frozen clean-validation choice remains practically competitive under many non-target observation degradations and across most unseen Beijing stations. The target-history checkpoint then identifies a clear boundary: sustained PM$_{2.5}$ outages can produce a meaningful affected-window selection penalty even when the full-period average remains reassuring. The resulting contribution is therefore a bounded empirical argument about when a simple clean-selected model remains defensible, and when that conclusion begins to fail.
